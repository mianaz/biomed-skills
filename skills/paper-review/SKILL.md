# Paper Review

## Establish the review scope

Read [writing principles](../biomed-shared/writing-principles.md). Identify the
scientific question and the contribution the manuscript seeks to establish.
Use the designated draft and matching figures, legends, Methods and supplements.
Reuse any framing brief, evidence map, claim IDs and source locators. Inspect the
evidence needed for consequential findings; a full evidence graph is optional.
Record the files and sections actually reviewed and material access gaps once.

Choose the requested mode, or infer it from the task:

| Mode | Focus |
|---|---|
| Logic | Scientific question, experiment purpose, argument progression, mechanism bridges and conceptual consistency. |
| Scientific, the default for general critique | Logic plus study design, comparisons, controls, analysis and inferential support. |
| Pre-submission | Scientific review across the current manuscript, with numerical, figure, source and version consistency. |

Review a supplied section at that scope. Journal requirements and package
preparation belong to `submission-check`; systematically checking cited claims
against external sources belongs to `citation-audit`. Complete any such products
the user requests and reuse their records. A scientific review can identify
specific citation risks without certifying every reference.

## Assess the argument and evidence

Use [evidence and interpretation](../biomed-shared/evidence-and-interpretation.md)
and [scientific semantics](../biomed-shared/scientific-semantics.md) for claim
support and measured objects. Use [argument bridges](../biomed-shared/argument-bridges.md)
for hidden premises, detached lists and unearned synthesis. For a structural
problem, use the relevant [section responsibilities](../biomed-shared/section-responsibilities.md)
and [paragraph guidance](../biomed-shared/paragraphs-and-revision.md).

At manuscript level, check whether the question, central discovery and explanation
agree with the title, abstract and figure sequence. Identify the scientific role
of each major experiment: what it establishes, distinguishes or extends. Find
important insights left implicit as well as unsupported leaps. Compare novelty
with the closest primary work only after reading the relevant sources; keep an
unverified comparison provisional.

In scientific and pre-submission modes, check the design relevant to each
consequential claim: independent unit, pairing or nesting, comparators, controls,
confounding, endpoint, effect and uncertainty, and analysis/multiplicity where
they affect inference. Examine a competing explanation when it could materially
change the conclusion. Identify the actual evidence that distinguishes it or the
specific unresolved inference. Evaluate descriptive, mechanistic, methods and
resource studies by their scientific purpose.

Distinguish a contradiction that undermines the central interpretation from an
open mechanistic question that extends it. An incomplete mechanism can support
an interesting proposed model. Explain its value and qualify the precise open
link. Propose a new experiment only when its result is needed for a stated claim
or when future experiments are requested; offer a repair from existing evidence
when one is available.

For cross-manuscript consistency, use
[revision fidelity](../biomed-shared/revision-fidelity.md) to reconcile current
values and source versions. Locate both conflicting statements. Resolve against
the owning figure or source table when possible; leave unresolved choices clear.

## Report actionable findings

Lead with a brief assessment of the contribution and the most consequential
issue, if one exists. Order findings by their effect on the scientific conclusion:

- **P0 — Central conclusion at risk:** a direct contradiction or fundamental
  design/analysis problem prevents the main inference as written.
- **P1 — Major revision:** a consequential claim, comparison, premise or source
  relationship needs repair, reanalysis or targeted verification.
- **P2 — Local revision:** a bounded wording, placement or consistency problem
  has a concrete effect on interpretation.

For each finding give the manuscript location and claim, the evidence location
or specific missing source, the problem and its impact, a feasible repair, and a
resolution check. Reuse existing IDs. For repeated material, locate both passages.
Use compact entries or a table suited to the review size. Separate verified
findings from items that need source access; unavailable material alone does not
establish a scientific flaw. Report no material findings when the reviewed scope
supports that result. Avoid quotas and generic demands for more validation.

The deliverable is the review and prioritized repairs. Whole-manuscript rewriting
requires a writing request. Review directly in one pass; do not recursively
launch reviewers or the drafting workflow. For an internal writing review,
return findings to the primary writer, who owns edits. Finish when each reported
issue has an evidence pointer, an interpretive consequence and a checkable repair.

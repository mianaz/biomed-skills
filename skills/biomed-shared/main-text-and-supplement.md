# Main text and supplementary material

Place each result according to the scientific inference it contributes. Keep the
central discovery, decisive comparisons, necessary controls and findings that
change the central interpretation visible in the main text. Give their direction,
magnitude and uncertainty where needed, with accurate references to full data.

Extended procedures, secondary analyses, additional specifications and repeated
support for the same conclusion can go in Methods or supplementary material.
A new condition belongs in the main argument when it reveals a biological
boundary, excludes an important alternative or establishes a distinct consequence.
Preserve required reporting and primary outcomes when reorganizing a paper.

After adding text, identify the new sentence's purpose and combine or replace
existing text that serves the same purpose. A response letter may explain a
reviewer's concern fully while the manuscript receives the concise clarification
its readers need. Check the destination and cross-reference of every moved item.

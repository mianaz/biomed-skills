# Choosing the next experiment

Use when the user asks for follow-up experiments, project priorities or extensions
of a paper. Begin with the most consequential open biological question, the current
evidence and informative alternative explanations. Preserve the conceptual value
of the finding while developing how it could change understanding.

Recommend a small set of experiments appropriate to the user's time and resources.
For each priority, connect the competing predictions to a feasible comparison and
explain how the plausible outcomes would change the model or the next decision.
Use orthogonal interventions or measurements where they resolve the uncertainty;
build on experiments already completed. Consider a small exploratory experiment
when an unexpected observation opens an interesting direction.

Prioritize the information gained, scientific importance and feasibility. Give
the user the recommended next step and its rationale. The relationship may fit
in prose or a compact experiment / predictions / outcome implications table.
Use `biomed-stats` for requested allocation, sample-size or analysis
planning and `paper-to-protocol` for an executable procedure from a source paper.
For a full biological experiment plan, `experiment-design` adds controls,
resources, pilot decisions and outcome interpretation when available. A new
research direction belongs to `research-ideation`; `research-project` handles
the adopted queue, calendar and root dashboard. Keep a short follow-up answer
at the requested scale.

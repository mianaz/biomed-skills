# Revision fidelity

Use when editing developed prose, including manuscript text rebuilt by
paper-writing. The requested edit scope governs the work. Correct sentences can
stay unchanged; fluency edits should preserve the author's scientific voice.

## Match the edit to the request

| Scope | Working approach |
|---|---|
| Minimal, the default for local polishing | Preserve accurate sentence backbones and order. Repair a missing link with a short clause, appositive or connected sentence. |
| Structural | Reorder sentences or paragraphs to develop the argument; give a scientific reason for consequential moves. |
| Substantive | Develop the argument from supplied evidence. Identify new scientific claims or proposed explanations in the edit notes. |

For minimal edits within one language, aim to keep growth within roughly 15–20%
of the original passage; compression requests use their requested length. This
is a local editing preference, not a universal sentence rule or a requirement to
add words. Check the passage's word count when this target applies. Before
exceeding it, integrate missing premises into existing clauses and remove a
repeated topic or closing statement. If a necessary repair still needs more
space, deliver the concise repair and explain the consequential addition briefly.
Preserve needed reasoning and facts.
Translation and full restructuring need a scope check rather than a cross-language
word-count ratio. User-specified limits take precedence.

## Compare meaning before and after

Read the original and revision proposition by proposition. Use
[scientific semantics](scientific-semantics.md) for the subject, measured object,
conditions, comparison, direction, negation, quantifiers and causal status.
Check values, units, denominators, independent sample units, terminology,
citation keys and figure references. Preserve meaningful statistical usage such
as “significantly” when a reported test supports it.

For example, “no evidence of harm at the tested dose” cannot become “the treatment
is safe,” and “not solely explained by X” cannot become “not explained by X.”
Preserve the scope of the original proposition when improving either sentence.
If the source itself is inconsistent or ambiguous, flag the exact issue and
revise the parts the evidence resolves. Supply no plausible missing number.

Check additions as carefully as deletions. Distinguish an explanation already
present in the evidence from a new biological claim. Local polishing uses the
former. Develop new models when substantive writing is within the requested
scope, with source support and a clear inferential status.

## Check the current manuscript

Use the designated manuscript and its matching figures, source tables and
analysis version. For a full revision, check consequential numbers and terms
across the title, abstract, Results, legends and supplement. If versions disagree,
record both locations and resolve against the owning source. A newer timestamp
alone does not settle the scientific choice. Leave unresolved values visible
for an author decision while completing unaffected edits.

Deliver the revised prose first. Add only consequential notes: a changed claim,
a needed source, an unresolved conflict or a material scope/length change.
Keep detailed comparison records only when requested or needed for a substantial
revision. Completion is usable prose with scientific meaning and contribution
preserved.

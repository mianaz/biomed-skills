# Revision fidelity

Use when editing developed prose. Preserve the author's scientific meaning and
ambition. Keep effective sentences and distinctive expressions, including useful
directness, emphasis and rhythm. Change them for a specific gain in clarity,
focus or precision.

## Match the requested scope

- **Minimal, the local-polishing default:** retain effective structures and order;
  repair wording or a missing link locally.
- **Structural:** reorder sentences or paragraphs for a stated scientific reason.
- **Substantive:** develop the argument from supplied evidence; note new claims
  or proposed explanations.

For minimal edits within one language, aim to keep growth within roughly 15–20%
and check the word count. Integrate necessary additions into existing sentences
and remove repetition first. Deliver a longer necessary repair with a brief
explanation. User limits take precedence; translation and full restructuring use
their own scope rather than a cross-language word-count ratio.

## Compare meaning

Check the subject, measured object, comparison, conditions, direction, numbers,
units, independent sample unit, terminology, citations and figure references.
Preserve meaningful statistical wording such as “significantly.” Check additions
as carefully as deletions: local polishing clarifies existing meaning; substantive
writing can develop sourced models at their stated status.

For ambiguity or changes in an assay, negation, quantifier or causal scope, read
[scientific semantics](scientific-semantics.md). Resolve what the evidence permits
and identify any factual choice that still needs the author.

For multiple sections, source versions or conflicting values, read
[manuscript consistency](manuscript-consistency.md).

Return usable prose first, with only consequential notes as described in the
writing principles. Keep detailed comparison records when requested or needed
for a substantial revision.

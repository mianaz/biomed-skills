# Terminology and citations

Reuse the project's accepted names, abbreviations, gene/protein distinctions,
units and statistical terminology. For a substantial manuscript, maintain a short
term list from the source and apply it across text, figures and legends. A local
edit needs a check of the affected terms and dependent statements.

Keep scientific referents and technical distinctions stable. Equivalent wording,
accepted short forms and varied sentence structures can improve natural prose
when the reader can still follow the same entity and proposition. Check concept
collisions as well as synonym
drift: a term may refer to different measurements in adjacent sentences, and two
terms may have been treated as interchangeable. For example, exposure duration
and systemic drug exposure describe different quantities. Choose a replacement
for its meaning and fit within the passage. Use
[scientific semantics](scientific-semantics.md) when the measured object or
inference changes with the wording.

When a term first carries an inference, supply its relevant property through
the example or a short explanatory clause. Keep a synthesis within the concepts
the paragraph has developed; use [argument bridges](argument-bridges.md) for a
missing premise or an unsupported summary dimension. A requested glossary can
define terms separately.

Preserve numbers, citation keys, labels and figure references when revising. Use
sources actually read and attach each citation to the statement it supports.
Check the cited passage's population, comparison, endpoint and inference against
the manuscript claim. Source identity and correct formatting are separate from
claim support. Mark missing source details explicitly; an unavailable full text
leaves its support unverified. A requested systematic claim-to-source check
belongs to `citation-audit`; ordinary polishing preserves references and flags
specific issues encountered at that scope.

Verify a named journal's current official
instructions before stating format, length or submission requirements; record the
source and date. General writing examples convey editorial patterns.

# Reader read-back

Use within the task's existing final text or figure review. Read the delivered
view at its intended size and scope: the passage with necessary neighboring text,
or the figure with its caption. State briefly what question it addresses, what
comparison or finding is apparent, and why that finding matters. For a figure,
also identify what attracts attention first and how its encodings are read.

For a whole manuscript, first read the title, abstract, Results headings and
figure titles as a short version of the paper. Restate its central contribution;
then check the full text for compatible promises and distinct advances. In a
structural review, identify what each paragraph lets the reader understand next.

When using an independent reviewer, give it this view and the intended audience
first, in a fresh context without the writer's history or explanatory brief.
Obtain its read-back before supplying the intended message and supporting evidence.
Then continue the same review with those materials. A direct separate pass can
check the visible information when independent feedback is unavailable; describe
that pass accurately if asked. Use real reader feedback when available.

Compare the read-back with the scientific purpose and evidence. Repair the specific
source of a material mismatch: emphasis, missing context, comparator, terminology,
panel order or encoding. An unfamiliar chart may need a short reading cue or direct
label. Recheck the affected passage or view after the repair.

Keep a consequential misunderstanding and its repair in the existing review notes.
The user receives the requested text or figure and useful notes at that scope.

# Paragraphs and revision

Give each paragraph one clear contribution, developed through evidence, comparison
and interpretation. When a result or experiment appears, supply the context needed
to understand its comparison and purpose. Context, content and conclusion are
reading functions; the reasoning determines the paragraph's shape.

## Subjects, voice and rhythm

Choose the subject from the paragraph's focal object and the preceding sentence.
Name concrete cells, treatments, measurements or results; replace abstract noun
chains with clear actors and actions. Lead from familiar information into the new
finding; put the scientific point in the main clause and keep its subject and verb
easy to connect. Resolve an ambiguous “this” with a specific noun. Keep useful
repetition of an established technical term. Establish the comparator, condition and
metric before interpreting a change. Preserve values, units, reference groups and
direction; identify a missing comparator rather than inventing it.

Active or passive voice should carry the focus forward while preserving actor,
recipient and causal direction. Vary sentence structure and length with the
reasoning: a short sentence can emphasize a finding; a longer one can hold a
connected comparison. Keep effective long sentences and meaningful qualifications.
Split multiple reasoning steps when doing so makes the relationship clearer.

For concrete examples of focus-led subject and voice choices, read
[sentence examples](sentence-focus-examples.md) when those choices need illustration.

## Forward movement

Make each paragraph add a finding, inference or question. End at its last
substantive sentence when the point is complete. A closing sentence earns its
place through a specific biological consequence, mechanism or unresolved question.
Terms such as “opens opportunities” need the actual opportunity and its meaning.

Check neighboring paragraphs for repeated motivation, findings or conclusions.
Give a structural move a scientific reason; combine text serving the same purpose.
Continuity can come from subjects, information order, shared terms or a question
followed by its answer. Add a bridge only for needed reasoning or orientation.
For a hidden premise or an example-to-judgment gap, use
[argument bridges](argument-bridges.md).

Preserve established terms, scientific ambition and technical meaning. When
turning Chinese notes into English prose, reconstruct the argument naturally and
retain figures, quantities and scientific intent. Explain consequential changes
in the user's requested language.

## Structural revision

For structural or substantive revision, settle the contribution and evidence order
before paragraph structure, then sentence detail and cross-references. Reuse the
working outline or figures. Draft sections in a useful order; align the title and
abstract with the finished argument.

# Paragraphs and revision

Give each paragraph a clear message. Its evidence, comparison and interpretation
can work together to develop that message. Make the relationship between
sentences explicit through scientific reasoning. Use substantive subjects and
concrete verbs. Preserve numerical and technical meaning during compression.

Reuse established terminology across sections. Revise the passages implicated
by the user's request and any adjacent statements needed for consistency. Give
a structural change a scientific reason. After an addition, check whether an
existing passage already performs the same function and combine them where
appropriate. Preserve the ambition of the original idea while repairing its
logic, specificity or inferential scope.

For Chinese notes intended for an English manuscript, reconstruct the argument
in natural biomedical English. Preserve the author's scientific intent, figures,
quantities and terminology. Explain consequential changes in the language the
user requested.

## Concrete sentences and forward movement

Name the cell population, treatment, measurement or result doing the work of the
sentence. Replace chains of abstract nouns with an actor, an action and its
object. Introduce the comparator and condition before asking the reader to
interpret a change; name the metric that increased or decreased. Preserve the
original values, units, reference group and direction. Query missing comparisons
instead of supplying a plausible one.

Split a sentence when it carries several reasoning steps: observation, local
inference and wider implication can occupy connected sentences. Keep a needed
qualification beside its claim. Do not impose a word limit or break a comparison
into fragments that make its meaning harder to follow.

Give each paragraph a distinct contribution. Its closing sentence should add a
specific inference, mechanism or unresolved question only when that advances the
argument. End at the last substantive sentence when the point is already complete.
Remove endings such as “opens opportunities,” “complementary strategies” or
“different responses” when they merely rename what the paragraph has explained.
Keep these terms when the prose specifies the opportunity, strategy or response
and develops its biological consequence. Do not replace one stock ending with
another or force a prediction into every paragraph.

Check neighboring paragraphs for repeated motivation, findings and conclusions.
Introduce a motivation where it first becomes necessary; later paragraphs should
use it to advance the argument. Build transitions from the specific question or
inference that connects the paragraphs. Adding “Furthermore” or “Together” does
not repair a missing scientific connection.

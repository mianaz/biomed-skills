# Scientific semantics

Use for consequential claim wording in drafting, revision or review. Check the
dimensions implicated by the passage against the actual assay, design and source.
The unit of checking is the scientific proposition: subject, measured object,
comparison, conditions, direction, quantifier, negation and inferential strength.

## Preserve the measured object

| Distinction | Check in the passage |
|---|---|
| Gene, transcript and protein | Name what changed: transcript abundance, protein abundance, localization or another measured property. |
| Receptor abundance, activity and dependence | Expression, a functional readout and response to perturbation each answer a different question. |
| Reporter and endogenous response | Keep the reporter's identity visible; establish endogenous transcription from the relevant measurements. |
| Phenotype and functional mechanism | Identify the assay endpoint. Cell count, viability, proliferation and apoptosis need their corresponding measurements. |
| State program and cell function | A transcriptional state can motivate a functional model; functional measurements establish the tested activity. |
| Drug interaction and added benefit | Retain the interaction model and reference. Check additional inhibition or benefit from the corresponding comparison. |
| Statistical difference and equivalence | Preserve estimates, uncertainty and the actual test. Equivalence depends on a specified margin and suitable analysis. |
| Cells, cultures, donors and patients | Keep the independent unit, nesting and pairing. Distinguish measured cells from biological replicates. |

These are diagnostic examples, not required wording or a fixed validation
inventory. Use [evidence and interpretation](evidence-and-interpretation.md) for
association, intervention, necessity, sufficiency, mediation and direct binding.

## Preserve the proposition

Check which population and condition a statement covers. Retain dose, time,
treatment history, comparator and model when they change its meaning. Keep
partial and complete negation distinct: “not solely through X” permits a role for
X. Preserve the difference between an absence of evidence and evidence of absence.
Keep “some,” “all,” “only” and their scope accurate.

Check chronology separately from causation. Complementary experiments can
jointly support a model; their order in a paragraph supplies no causal link.
Keep a prediction, reconstruction or planned analysis at its stated status.

Use precise positive wording for the supported finding and its meaning. Retain
a necessary negative result, condition or qualifier where it changes the
conclusion. Removing defensive rhetoric should strengthen the scientific
statement while preserving this information.

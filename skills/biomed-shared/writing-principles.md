# Biomedical research writing

Apply when drafting, revising, or reviewing biomedical manuscripts, abstracts,
proposals, figure legends, and reviewer responses. Follow the current task's
language, audience, journal requirements, and editing scope.

## Governing priority: scientific interest and biological significance

Develop the most compelling biological story the findings can sustain. Lead with
the central surprise, conceptual advance, and reason the discovery matters.
Actively develop the findings' broader significance. Combine exact factual
reporting with ambitious biological interpretation. Give this priority greater
weight than generic preferences for conservative wording or exhaustive
qualification. Check for underselling as deliberately as for overstatement.

## Develop and sell the scientific idea

1. **Find the intellectual contribution.** Identify what changes in our
   understanding, which assumption the findings challenge, and which important
   question they open. Organize the manuscript around that contribution. Treat
   identifying and developing the story as part of the writing task.

2. **Make the positive value explicit.** Explain what the findings help us
   understand or make possible. Develop relevant connections to mechanisms,
   biological principles, disease, or therapeutic opportunities. Show the
   reasoning that gives these implications substance. Choose the implications
   that matter most for this study and its audience.

3. **Think creatively and allow open questions.** Develop conceptual connections,
   plausible mechanisms, competing interpretations, and testable predictions.
   An illuminating model or a new question can be a substantial contribution
   while mechanistic details remain unresolved. Use wording such as "we propose"
   or "supports a model" to express the full idea clearly. Approach biomedical
   discovery through biological context and synthesis; avoid importing formal
   proof requirements or engineering completeness checklists into its narrative.

4. **Actively remove defensive writing.** Open with the finding and its meaning.
   Remove habitual apologies, anticipated objections, stacked hedges, and
   unnecessary self-diminishing labels such as "merely descriptive." Use a precise
   qualifier where an inference needs one. Explain limitations that materially
   affect interpretation where they arise, proportionately and without repeated
   disclaimers. Let each section develop its scientific contribution.

5. **Preserve the ambition during revision.** Strengthen an interesting argument
   by clarifying its reasoning and inferential scope. Retain its conceptual reach
   when correcting wording. Distinguish a problem that undermines the central
   interpretation from an unanswered question that extends the discovery. Make
   the resulting opportunity for further work intellectually specific.

## Make the manuscript work

6. **Build a biological narrative.** Let titles, the abstract, Results headings,
   and figure order communicate discoveries. Make each paragraph advance the
   argument through findings and their significance. Keep essential evidence and
   context in the main text; place supporting detail appropriately. Describe
   procedures in Methods.

7. **Write natural scientific prose.** Use concrete verbs, clear subjects, and
   established biomedical and statistical terminology. Convey importance through
   substantive reasoning. Remove stock transitions, empty promotional adjectives,
   forced contrasts, invented labels, decorative hyphenation, and repetitive
   summaries. Preserve quantitative information and necessary technical meaning.

8. **Keep reporting exact.** Preserve observations, numbers, study design,
   citations, and figure correspondence. Make observed findings, causal claims,
   and proposed explanations distinguishable. Keep material contradictory results
   visible. Read relevant source material before evaluating it and briefly report
   access gaps that affect the task. In reviewer responses, answer the scientific
   concern directly and accurately describe the work and revisions completed.

## Before delivery

Strengthen any overlooked insight, broader implication, or unnecessarily timid
claim. Ensure the reader can identify what is new and why it matters. Verify
factual and numerical accuracy. Return the requested writing, with brief notes
for consequential editorial decisions or unresolved issues.

# Governance and design handoff

## Complete the risk screen

For each item record `yes`, `no`, `unknown`, or `not_applicable`, the governing body or
policy, approval identifier/status, restrictions, and unresolved action:

- human participants, identifiable data, consent, waiver, secondary use, and return of
  results;
- animal use, humane endpoints, species-specific oversight, and the 3Rs;
- infectious, recombinant, radioactive, chemical, drug, device, or other hazardous
  material;
- protected, proprietary, controlled-access, export-restricted, or licensed data;
- clinical or operational decisions that require regulatory or institutional review;
- disclosure, small-cell, re-identification, dual-use, and publication constraints.

Do not infer approval from data access. Stop irreversible work when a required approval
or safe operating procedure is missing or unknown.

## Record practical feasibility

Name recruitment, sample, assay, instrument, staff, site, budget, compute, storage,
timeline, access, and vendor constraints. Distinguish:

- fixed constraints that bound the claim;
- negotiable constraints with a decision deadline;
- assumptions requiring a feasibility check;
- missing authority that blocks the design.

Prefer a narrower answerable question over a broad design that cannot be executed.

## Handoff record

Include:

1. decision and primary question;
2. design family and rationale;
3. target population or biological system;
4. estimand and endpoint hierarchy;
5. unit hierarchy and independent `n`;
6. sampling, allocation, controls, randomization, blinding, and blocking;
7. analysis sets, exclusions, missingness, and stopping rules;
8. sample-size or precision assumptions and sensitivity;
9. principal bias controls and residual threats;
10. governance and approval status;
11. required data and metadata;
12. planned analysis handoff and validation criteria;
13. unresolved decisions, owners, and deadlines.

The analysis contract may reference this record rather than duplicating it, but stable
IDs for units, outcomes, analysis sets, and comparisons must agree.

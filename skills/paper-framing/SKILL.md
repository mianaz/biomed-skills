# Paper Framing

## Prepare

Read [writing principles](../biomed-shared/writing-principles.md) before developing
the argument. Use [evidence and interpretation](../biomed-shared/evidence-and-interpretation.md)
when evaluating claims, mechanisms or novelty. Reuse the supplied figures, result
notes, manuscript and any existing claim IDs; inspect the sources needed for the
consequential decisions. Use the user's established audience and constraints.

The deliverable is the recommended scientific argument. For a request that also
asks for manuscript sections, complete the framing then use `paper-writing` for
those sections. A short illustrative title or significance paragraph can make a
framing recommendation concrete.

## 1. Identify the contribution

Explain the most consequential finding, what is surprising about it and what
changes in biological understanding. Compare candidate stories only when the
material supports a meaningful choice. Develop the strongest candidate through
its substantive advance and relevant literature. A descriptive, mechanistic,
methods or resource study should be judged by the scientific question it answers.

Complete this step with a central contribution sentence and a significance
paragraph explaining why the finding matters to the intended reader.

## 2. Develop the explanation

Connect complementary observations into a biological model. Examine whether
selectivity, heterogeneity or an apparent exception reveals a useful principle.
State the reasoning from observation to interpretation. For an unresolved
mechanism, develop the proposal and a prediction that distinguishes it from an
informative alternative. Read the relevant [biomedical example](../biomed-shared/biomedical-examples.md)
when the discovery is undersold or the mechanism is incomplete.

Complete this step when the model explains the important findings and its
established relationships and proposed links can be distinguished.

## 3. Arrange claims and figures

For each necessary subclaim, identify the scientific question, decisive evidence
and source, inference gained and next question. Reuse an existing evidence map
and stable IDs. Arrange the main figures to advance those inferences; the study
determines their count. Read [result placement](../biomed-shared/main-text-and-supplement.md)
when choosing main figures or moving supporting detail.

Complete this step when each proposed main figure has an identifiable role and
the reader can follow the central argument through the figure sequence.
Detailed graph construction belongs to `paper-evidence-map`; rendering, statistics
and panel appearance belong to `scientific-plotting`.

## 4. Deliver the framing

Use [the brief](assets/framing-brief.md) for a whole study. For a local question,
return the recommendation at that scale. Include the central story, scientific
significance, model, essential subclaims with source pointers and overall figure
sequence. Explain a consequential choice or unresolved factual issue briefly.

Check whether an interesting implication remains implicit or an established
finding is undersold. Check the recommendation against the actual evidence.
When new experiments are unavailable, finish the strongest framing supported by
the existing work; include feasible reanalysis or proposed future tests only to
the extent requested. Completion is a usable framing recommendation.

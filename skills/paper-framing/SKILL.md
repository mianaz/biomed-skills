# Paper Framing

## Governing priority: scientific interest and biological significance

Develop the most compelling biological story the findings can sustain. Lead with
the central surprise, conceptual advance and reason the discovery matters.
Actively develop the positive scientific value: what the findings change in our
understanding, which assumption they challenge, what they help explain and what
new possibilities they open. Give this priority greater weight than generic
preferences for conservative wording or exhaustive qualification. Check for
underselling as deliberately as for overstatement.

Treat the development of the scientific idea as part of the task. Combine exact
reporting with ambitious interpretation, biological reasoning and creative
synthesis. Develop plausible mechanisms, competing explanations, conceptual
models and testable predictions. Express a substantive proposed model clearly
with wording such as “we propose” or “supports a model”. An illuminating model or
an important new question can contribute substantially while individual
mechanistic details remain unresolved.

Open with the finding and its meaning. Remove habitual apologies, anticipated
objections, stacked hedges and self-diminishing labels. Attach uncertainty to
the particular inference that needs it. Address a limitation where it changes
interpretation, proportionately and once. Preserve conceptual ambition when
correcting wording. Distinguish a challenge to the central interpretation from
an unanswered question that extends the discovery.

## Work at the requested scale

Use the user's language, audience, journal, materials and editing scope. Read
supplied manuscripts and figures before judging their story; inspect the actual
panels, legends and relevant Methods. Reuse earlier decisions and reading notes,
checking consequential claims against their sources. State an access gap once
when it affects the assessment. A partial paper supports an assessment of the
accessible material.

For a local edit, deliver the revised passage with only consequential notes. For
framing or restructuring a whole paper, follow the workflow below and use the
bundled templates when helpful. An audit request calls for an assessment; a
writing request calls for the requested prose. Continue from the available
material with explicit placeholders for missing facts. Ask only about a choice
that materially affects the argument and cannot be resolved from context.

## 1. Find the intellectual contribution

Identify the biological problem and the most consequential finding. Explain why
an informed reader might be surprised, what previously plausible view changes,
and what becomes understandable or possible. Read the closest relevant work
before making a firm novelty comparison; identify the actual distinction in
question, system, explanation, capability or evidence. Support literature claims
with sources actually read. Keep an unverified novelty comparison provisional.

Write one clear sentence stating the central contribution and its scientific
meaning. Use substantive language rather than promotional adjectives. Develop
a short significance paragraph that makes the reasoning explicit. Connect to
mechanism, biological organization, disease or therapeutic opportunity when the
study supports the connection; basic biology can carry its own significance.

For a whole study with several plausible stories, compare up to three candidate
framings. Explain the scientific insight, the evidence that makes each compelling
and the audience it speaks to. Recommend one and develop it. A clear existing
story needs refinement rather than an obligatory menu of alternatives.

## 2. Develop the biological explanation

Synthesize the findings into an explanation of how the system behaves. Identify
relationships across experiments, conditions, cell states or scales. Consider
whether a surprising difference, selective response or apparent exception
reveals an organizing principle.

Make the bridge from observation to interpretation explicit: what was measured,
what that measurement establishes and why it supports the proposed explanation.
Use biological context to develop the idea further. When several explanations
remain plausible, state the most informative models and a prediction that would
distinguish them. Label an author hypothesis, a literature finding and a new
interpretation accurately.

Evaluate the contribution according to its scientific purpose. Mechanistic
studies can explain regulation; descriptive studies can reveal previously hidden
organization; methods can enable a consequential measurement or discovery;
resources and benchmarks can expose biological variation or conditions that
change scientific decisions. Select relevant standards for the study rather
than imposing a fixed validation inventory.

## 3. Build the scientific argument and figure sequence

Choose the subclaims that advance the central idea. For each, identify the
question answered, decisive observation, source figure or analysis, biological
interpretation and next question. Use a compact table when it helps the author
make decisions. Preserve exact measurements, comparisons, sample units,
quantitative results and citations.

Arrange the main figures so that each supplies an inference needed by the
emerging story. A useful progression may move from a phenomenon to its source,
regulation, consequences and wider significance; choose the order supported by
this study. A different paper may lead with a new capability or an unexpected
comparison. Let the scientific argument determine the sequence.

Give each main figure a finding and a role in the paper. Explain what the reader
would fail to understand without it. Combine repeated demonstrations when they
establish the same conclusion. Keep essential controls and results that change
the central interpretation visible in the main text. Place extended methods,
secondary analyses and supporting detail in accessible supplements, with accurate
cross-references. Choose placement by scientific function and reporting needs.

## 4. Write the manuscript from its discoveries

Let the title, abstract, Results headings and figure order communicate the same
central advance. Use concrete verbs, clear subjects and established biomedical
and statistical terminology. Give each paragraph a clear contribution to the
argument; connect its observations and interpretation through explicit reasoning.

In Results, state what the experiments establish and include interpretation that
answers the local biological question. In Discussion, synthesize across findings,
relate the proposed understanding to prior work, and develop broader implications
and productive open questions. Use [Section writing](references/section-writing.md)
for the sections requested. Complete the requested writing rather than stopping
at an outline unless the user requested outline approval.

For a named journal, adapt the scientific pitch to its audience. Verify current
journal and article-type instructions before stating formatting requirements.
Treat general editorial patterns as writing guidance. Explain the contribution
with the actual data even when submission plans change.

## 5. Revise with scientific ambition intact

Look deliberately for valuable insights left implicit: an unexpected connection,
a challenged assumption, a coherent mechanism, an informative heterogeneity or
a new research direction. Develop the strongest of these in the prose. Replace
vague claims of importance with their biological explanation.

Correct an overbroad statement by identifying the supported relationship and
context, then state that result confidently. Preserve strong observed findings
when qualifying a downstream inference. For an unresolved mechanism, articulate
the full proposed model and its discriminating prediction. Remove repeated
qualifiers, empty transitions, forced contrasts, invented terminology and
unnecessary summaries.

When the user has no time or resources for additional experiments, complete the
strongest manuscript supported by the existing work. Consider useful reanalysis
or synthesis of available results within the requested scope. Put optional
future experiments after the completed deliverable when requested, and explain
which scientific question each would address.

## 6. Check the finished work

Read the title, abstract, headings and figure titles together. Can a reader
identify the discovery and why it matters? Does the Discussion offer a
substantive interpretation? Strengthen anything interesting that remains buried
or unnecessarily timid.

Then check facts, numbers, comparisons, sample units, statistical wording,
terminology, references and figure correspondence. Keep material contradictory
findings visible. Preserve distinctions between association, intervention,
necessity, sufficiency, direct interaction and clinical implication according to
the study design. Preserve the status of exploratory analyses and proposed
explanations. Never invent results, approvals, completed experiments or sources.

For a whole-paper framing task, normally deliver a recommended central story,
a significance paragraph, the necessary subclaims with their evidence, a figure
sequence and the requested writing. For a local task, deliver the passage.
Include brief notes only for consequential editorial decisions, missing facts
or unresolved choices. Keep working inventories out of the manuscript.

# Writing the biomedical manuscript

Use the sections needed for the request. Adapt the structure to the study and
current article type. The patterns below are editorial guidance.

## Title

Name the discovery or the capability that changes what scientists can study.
Make the important biological relationship visible. Choose specific terms that
the intended reader recognizes. A mechanistic verb must match the intervention
and controls; a proposed mechanism may be introduced in the text at its proper
scope. Avoid a title whose only information is the technique or broad topic.

Offer a few meaningfully different titles when requested: one can emphasize the
biological insight, another the conceptual model and another the new capability.
Explain a consequential difference in what each promises. Preserve the study's
actual contribution in every version.

## Abstract

Orient the reader to a consequential biological problem. Identify the unresolved
question that this study answers. Introduce the approach briefly, then give the
central finding and the supporting result needed to understand its meaning.
Develop the final implication: what changes in our understanding or what becomes
possible. Include essential quantitative information when it helps establish the
contribution and is available in the source.

Lead the result sentences with discoveries. Keep routine procedural detail in
Methods. Qualify a particular inference locally; choose the final sentence for
the scientific insight or supported opportunity. Include a material limitation
where it affects the interpretation. Follow the actual journal's abstract format
and length when verified.

## Introduction

Start with the biological phenomenon or problem that gives the study purpose.
Bring the reader to the tension in current understanding: an unexplained
observation, competing mechanisms, a missing distinction or a measurement that
prevents an important question from being answered.

Use the closest literature to establish that tension. Explain how the present
question follows from what is already known. Give prior contributions their
actual credit and verify claims about what has not been studied. Describe the
specific advance of this study in the closing paragraph, at a level of detail
appropriate to the journal. Align that advance with the Results that follow.

Define specialist terminology near first use when writing for a broad audience.
A short, focused opening can carry more biological motivation than a catalogue
of disease burden, pathway names and techniques.

## Results

Organize each subsection around a biological question and the inference gained
from answering it. A typical unit introduces the question, provides the key
comparison and observation, and explains what the evidence establishes. The
next unit follows from a remaining question or extends the finding's meaning.

Use headings that communicate findings. Report effect directions, quantitative
results, uncertainty and sample units accurately. Explain unusual or decisive
design features when the reader needs them to interpret the result; place
routine execution detail in Methods. Tie citations and figure panels to the
statements they support.

Include local interpretation when it makes the experiment intelligible. For
example, a selective response can establish context dependence, and a controlled
perturbation can support a regulatory role. Reserve extended integration with
other studies and broad mechanistic proposals for Discussion when they require
several findings or additional reasoning.

Adjacent subsections should advance distinct inferences. A second measurement
can justify a separate subsection when it changes the explanation or establishes
an independent consequence. Additional measurements that support the same point
can be grouped with the first result or placed in supplementary material.

## Figure sequence and legends

For each proposed main figure, write its question, finding, decisive panels,
interpretation and reason it belongs at that position. Use the fewest figures
that convey the substantive discoveries clearly. The study determines the count.
A phenomenon, source, intervention, consequence and synthesis sequence is one
possible structure; use a capability, comparison or resource structure when it
better expresses the contribution.

A legend should orient the reader to the scientific comparison and explain each
panel in order. Include group definitions, sample units, n, summaries, error bars,
tests, multiplicity handling and exclusions when relevant and supported. Keep
terminology, conditions and figure references consistent. A conceptual model
must distinguish established observations from proposed links. A diagram can
summarize an interpretation; its arrows do not supply experimental evidence.

For a figure audit, inspect the rendered panels at their intended size. Check
label readability, condition colors, legends, symbols, scales, image adjustments,
statistics and correspondence to source data. Exact dimensions and journal
formatting must come from the relevant current instructions or the user's
specified design. Report the checks actually performed.

## Methods

Describe what was done, in a logical sequence that allows the reader to
understand the design and reproduce the work. Preserve actual conditions,
independent units, controls, quantities, reagent identifiers, software, analysis
choices and statistical methods. Cite adapted methods and specify modifications.
Use concise narrative prose with clear subjects and verbs.

A planned protocol supplies a template until execution records establish what
was performed. Keep missing factual details in author questions, using a draft
placeholder where necessary. Explain a design choice when it is needed to
understand the scientific inference. A conventional method can be essential to
an original biological discovery without needing to be presented as a new method.

## Discussion

Open with the central discovery and the view of the biological system that it
supports. Combine the findings into an explanation that goes beyond their
individual descriptions. Show how this explanation extends, resolves or changes
specific prior ideas, using the literature for substantive scientific reasoning.

Develop the strongest implications supported by that reasoning. Depending on
the study, these may concern regulation, state transitions, tissue organization,
disease mechanisms, therapeutic opportunities or what can now be measured.
Explain the steps connecting the findings to each implication. Retain the
conceptual reach when some mechanistic links remain proposals.

For competing explanations, state the informative differences and a prediction
that separates them. Treat an unresolved mechanism as a precise scientific
question. Address a limitation in the paragraph whose interpretation it affects:
name the relevant design feature, the inference it qualifies and what would
resolve it. A study need not end with a generic list of limitations.

Close with the resulting understanding or an intellectually specific opportunity
for further work. Give the final paragraph a scientific purpose.

## Cover letter and significance paragraph

State the discovery, the important question it answers and the audience that
benefits from the result. Explain the conceptual advance with one or two decisive
findings. Relate the study to the target journal's actual scope. Support novelty
comparisons with literature that has been read. Keep administrative declarations
accurate to author-provided information and the requested submission stage.

## Paragraphs and revision

Give each paragraph a clear message. Its evidence, comparison and interpretation
can work together to develop that message. Make the relationship between
sentences explicit through scientific reasoning. Use substantive subjects and
concrete verbs. Preserve numerical and technical meaning during compression.

Reuse established terminology across sections. Revise the passages implicated
by the user's request and any adjacent statements needed for consistency. Give
a structural change a scientific reason. After an addition, check whether an
existing passage already performs the same function and combine them where
appropriate. Preserve the ambition of the original idea while repairing its
logic, specificity or inferential scope.

For Chinese notes intended for an English manuscript, reconstruct the argument
in natural biomedical English. Preserve the author's scientific intent, figures,
quantities and terminology. Explain consequential changes in the language the
user requested.

# Review design

## Choose the review type

| Type | Suitable aim | Required transparency |
|---|---|---|
| Systematic | Complete answer to a focused question | Protocol, comprehensive sources, duplicate screening when feasible, risk of bias, reproducible synthesis |
| Scoping | Map concepts, evidence types, or gaps | Broad framework, explicit search/screening, structured charting, no implied effect certainty |
| Rapid | Decision under a deadline | Prespecified shortcuts, at least one quality-control pass, explicit completeness limits |
| Structured narrative | Explain a bounded topic or reconcile mechanisms | Reproducible targeted search, source-selection rationale, claim-level citations |

Do not call an unlogged search systematic. A living review also needs a search-update
schedule, deduplication strategy, versioned corpus, and change policy.

## Frame the question

- Intervention: population, intervention, comparator, outcome, time, setting.
- Exposure/etiology: population, exposure, comparator, outcome, time.
- Diagnosis: population, index test, reference standard, target condition, setting.
- Prognosis: population, predictor/model, outcome, prediction time and horizon.
- Qualitative/implementation: population, phenomenon, context, perspective.

Define primary outcomes and minimally important distinctions before searching.

## Protocol fields

Record review ID, rationale, question, intended decision, review type, eligible designs,
populations, interventions/exposures/tests, comparators, outcomes, dates, languages,
publication status, information sources, query plan, screening and disagreement process,
extraction fields, risk-of-bias method, synthesis plan, certainty method, subgroup and
sensitivity plans, software, any automated or AI-assisted prioritization and its
validation/override policy, registration, and deviations.

Choose restrictions from relevance and feasibility. Explain the bias each restriction
may introduce.

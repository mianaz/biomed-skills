# Synthesis and certainty

## Decide whether to pool

Pool only when studies estimate a sufficiently common quantity. Compare populations,
designs, interventions/exposures/tests, outcome definitions, time horizons, effect
measures, and bias. Do not use a random-effects model to legitimize incompatible
questions.

When pooling is defensible:

- harmonize effect direction and units transparently;
- preserve study-level estimates and denominators;
- choose fixed or random effects from the inferential target, not heterogeneity alone;
- report confidence intervals and heterogeneity with their uncertainty;
- examine influential studies and prespecified subgroups;
- assess small-study effects only when the number and comparability of studies permit;
- distinguish study count from participant or experimental-unit count.

For diagnostic accuracy, paired sensitivity/specificity and threshold variation need
models that preserve their dependence. For time-to-event outcomes, do not treat odds,
risks, rates, and hazards as interchangeable.

## Structured qualitative synthesis

Group by the prespecified question and design. For each outcome report direction,
magnitude, precision, risk of bias, directness, consistency, and plausible reasons for
conflict. Avoid vote counting by statistical significance.

## Certainty

Rate certainty per consequential outcome, not per paper or review. Consider risk of
bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication/selective-reporting bias,
and any justified upgrading factors for the chosen framework. Preserve domain-level
judgments and rationale rather than only an overall label.

Use bounded conclusions:

- `supported`: sufficiently direct and credible evidence meets the criterion;
- `not_supported`: adequate evidence does not support the prespecified claim;
- `inconclusive`: power, bias, heterogeneity, coverage, or indirectness prevents an
  answer;
- `technical_failure`: search, access, extraction, or synthesis could not be completed.

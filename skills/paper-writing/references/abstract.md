# Abstract

Orient the reader to a consequential biological problem. Identify the unresolved
question that this study answers. Introduce the approach briefly, then give the
central finding and the supporting result needed to understand its meaning.
Develop the final implication: what changes in our understanding or what becomes
possible. Include essential quantitative information when it helps establish the
contribution and is available in the source.

Lead the result sentences with discoveries. Keep routine procedural detail in
Methods. Qualify a particular inference locally; choose the final sentence for
the scientific insight or supported opportunity. Include a material limitation
where it affects the interpretation. Follow the actual journal's abstract format
and length when verified.

# Narrative Reviews and Perspectives

Start from the question the article will resolve for its readers and the central
judgment it will develop. Establish the scope from the requested audience, sources
and article type. Use the closest existing reviews to establish why this synthesis
is useful now, then ground its judgments in the primary studies. Read the relevant
studies before making a firm comparison;
keep source IDs attached to the observations that inform the synthesis.

Organize the argument around mechanisms, controversies, organizing principles or
bottlenecks. Compare studies where their biological systems, interventions,
measurements or assumptions explain agreement and disagreement. Identify the
conditions under which a proposed synthesis holds, and develop what this changes
in the field's understanding. Use the shared
[argument bridges](../../biomed-shared/argument-bridges.md) for these connections.

Choose display items that add intellectual value: a conceptual model, comparison
of competing explanations, or table connecting approaches to the questions they
can answer. A proposed model should identify its source observations and the
relationships still to be tested. Use `scientific-visual-explainer` when rendering
the schematic itself is requested.

End with specific open questions, distinguishing predictions or technical advances
that could resolve the central problem. Follow the requested deliverable: an
outline for an outline request, developed prose for a writing request. Assess
whether each section advances the central judgment and makes its significance clear.

For a systematic review or meta-analysis, match the manuscript's search, selection
and synthesis claims to the supplied protocol and execution records, and check
the applicable reporting requirements for that study type.

# How RNA programs Cas9

Audience: biomedical reader new to the original Cas9 experiments.
Learning goal: connect RNA-guided DNA recognition and strand-specific cleavage
to the experiments that made Cas9 programmable.

| Scene | Objects and change | Teaching point | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 Assemble, 0–10 s | A crRNA and tracrRNA approach; paired regions align inside a functional Cas9 outline. | Both RNAs support target cleavage in this system. | Fig. 1A–B; RNA arrangement Fig. 1E. |
| 02 Recognize, 10–20 s | The guide approaches its complementary DNA strand; the displaced strand and adjacent PAM remain identifiable. Toggle the PAM comparison. | Guide complementarity and the PAM are distinct recognition requirements. | Fig. 4B–C and corresponding Results. |
| 03 Cut, 20–30 s | Cut marks appear on identified strands. Switch WT, D10A and H840A. | HNH and RuvC have different strand assignments. | Fig. 2A–B. |
| 04 Simplify, 30–40 s | A linker joins the crRNA 3′ end to the tracrRNA 5′ end without moving the guide region. | A designed single RNA can direct cleavage. | Fig. 5A–D. |

All artwork is original, using BioRender-style flat biological vectors within
the black 3Blue1Brown-style stage. The protein silhouette, spacing, RNA tails and time are
schematic, not structural or kinetic measurements. Cut marks teach strand
assignment, not exact positions. The PAM choice summarizes a qualitative
experiment; the animation does not predict efficiency. Scene 04 is a design
transformation, not an intracellular ligation reaction. No quantitative data
plot, generated image, experimental image or narrated video is included.

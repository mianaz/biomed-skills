# Graphical abstract

Choose the content before choosing icons. Identify the study context, the central
discovery or change, and its biological consequence. Decide which relationship
will let the intended audience understand that contribution at a glance.

Retain the entities and comparisons needed for that message. Collapse secondary
intermediate steps into named process modules; keep their underlying identities
and source relationships in the existing storyboard or scene model. Preserve
compartments, direction, intervention and the distinction between observed effects
and proposed mechanisms. Use the same scientific model as the detailed figure.

A useful summary can show a change in state, a contrast between conditions or a
new organizing principle. Let the discovery determine the composition and number
of elements. Give unfamiliar entities short functional labels where useful, and
reserve explanatory detail for the caption or the requested interactive view.
Apply the [artwork title and label rule](composition.md#titles-and-biological-labels).

For an embedded mini-plot, decide whether it communicates a measured result or
symbolizes a method. Route measured results through
[scientific-plotting](../../scientific-plotting/SKILL.md) with usable axes, units,
scale and evidence context; keep its native guides with the panel. A method symbol
can be schematic when identified as such. Compose the surrounding editable names
and relationship labels with the schematic. Simplifying the overview should keep
the meaning of each quantitative layer intact.

Inspect the final view with the shared
[reader read-back](../../biomed-shared/reader-feedback.md). The visible relationships
should convey what changed, its consequence and why the finding matters. Expand
any compression that changes the scientific meaning. Deliver the formats requested
using the normal schematic export workflow.

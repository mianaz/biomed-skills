# Research Ideation

Read [shared principles](../biomed-shared/core-principles.md).

Read [research principles](../biomed-shared/research-principles.md) and the
[author's scientific priorities](../biomed-shared/writing-principles.md).
Start from the supplied observations, current understanding and practical
resources. Explain what is surprising and what could change in biology.

## Develop possibilities

Make room for generative thinking before ranking. Useful starting points include
a reproducible anomaly, a disagreement between models, a boundary condition,
an old question newly accessible to measurement, or a connection across fields.
Develop the user's idea seriously. Use literature to sharpen its most interesting
form and reveal substantive overlap.

For each worthwhile direction, connect:

- The observation or knowledge tension and its source.
- A question whose answer changes understanding or enables something useful.
- A plausible biological explanation and an informative alternative.
- Different predictions under a feasible intervention or observation.
- The smallest useful first test, available resources and what its outcomes
  would change.

Use as many candidates as the task needs. A focused request may need one well
developed idea. Keep a speculative but high-value direction visible when its
first informative test is feasible. Exploratory work can begin with a question
and discovery strategy before a detailed mechanism is known.

## Compare with the closest work

Read the nearest primary studies. Compare their biological question, system,
intervention, endpoint and conclusion with the proposed contribution. State the
concrete advance and the consequence of any overlap. Use `literature-library`
for a broader field map or search. Describe novelty within the inspected sources;
keep unresolved source access beside the affected comparison.

## Recommend a direction

Explain the recommendation through scientific interest, discriminating information,
feasibility, resource cost and time to a useful result. Show consequential tradeoffs
in a short table. Avoid invented success probabilities or a universal weighted
score. Use explicit user weights when they are requested.

Deliver the strongest question, working model, closest-work comparison and first
test. Retain promising alternatives with the condition that would make them worth
revisiting. State how an informative contrary or mixed result could reshape the
model. Use `experiment-design` for a concrete experiment plan, `research-project`
for an adopted task queue and schedule, and `paper-framing` for the scientific
story of an existing set of results.

Save a reusable idea brief at the scale requested and link it from the
[project dashboard](../biomed-shared/project-dashboard.md) when working in a project.
